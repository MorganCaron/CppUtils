## Discover the documentation

CppUtils is structured around several major modular libraries (unit testing, networking, mesh topology) alongside a rich collection of specialized utilities. Modern C++20 modules allow you to import the entire suite at once (`import CppUtils;`) without inflating your final binary, as only the components actually consumed by your application are integrated at compile time.

## Library Philosophy

CppUtils is built upon uncompromising core principles:

<Card title="Rich & modular toolbox" icon="puzzle">
	Brings together core essentials (containers, concurrency, networking, parsing, unit tests, cryptography, etc.) to eliminate dependency proliferation. Powered by **C++ modules**, this breadth maximizes reusability with zero penalty:
	- **Granular or all-in-one**: import a specific submodule (`import CppUtils.Container;`) or the entire suite (`import CppUtils;`).
	- **Zero binary bloat**: only symbols actually invoked by your application are compiled into the final executable.
	- **Hermetic isolation & faster builds**: each module is compiled only once, drastically reducing build times compared to redundant preprocessor (`#include`) headers.
</Card>

<Card title="Unopinionated integration & design by contract" icon="heart">
	Zero architectural lock-in or contagion: CppUtils never attempts to dictate its types or colonize your codebase.
	- **Non-invasive design & zero contagion**: unlike invasive frameworks such as **Qt** that mandate proprietary meta-compilers (`moc`), closed types (`QString`, `QByteArray`), and heavy inheritance trees, CppUtils remains strictly non-invasive and introduces zero viral dependencies: the goal is never to make you dependent to lock you into an ecosystem, actively refusing any form of vendor lock-in.
	- **Design by contract**: APIs are constrained through standard concepts (`std::ranges::input_range`, `std::formattable`, `std::invocable`, etc.) rather than bound to rigid, imposed types: your containers propagate without defensive copying or conversion overhead (explore [design by contract with `auto` and concepts on my blog](https://morgancaron.github.io/articles/cpp/auto#abbreviated-function-template-depuis-c20)).
	- **Isolated adoption**: pick a single standalone component (`MultiKeyMap`, `UniqueLocker`) in complete autonomy without being forced to import the rest of the library.
</Card>

<Card title="C++26 cutting edge, zero legacy debt" icon="rocket">
	Continuously targets the latest language standard (**C++26**) to immediately deliver state-of-the-art capabilities: native modules, monadic operations, advanced concepts, enhanced safety, and reflection.
	- **Zero legacy compromise**: no artificial preservation of obsolete patterns or technical debt accumulation.
	- **Pragmatic transition**: targeted feature-test macros (`__cpp_lib_*`) ease compilation on recent compilers with partial C++26 standard library rollouts, without carrying obsolete legacy baggage.
	- **Future-proof architecture**: continuous refactorings to keep the codebase clean, robust, and modern.
</Card>

<Card title="Performance & modern best practices" icon="seti:cpp">
	Maximum performance without ever sacrificing clarity or safety:
	- **Readability & simplicity**: clean, transparent, and self-documenting APIs that remove accidental complexity without hidden magic.
	- **Uncompromising performance**: strict zero-cost abstractions, cache-friendly memory layouts, and aggressive compile-time evaluation (`constexpr`, `consteval`).
	- **Modern best practices**: systematic RAII resource management, strong typing enforced by concepts, and elimination of preprocessor macros (several of these [best practices are detailed on my blog](https://morgancaron.github.io/articles/cpp/)).
</Card>
